Colored & Grass Paver Mould Buyer Guide

Paver jobs fail on three things: picking a decorative shape for vehicle loads, laying hollow units without the right sub-base, and ignoring pattern direction on four-piece motifs. Get these right and the pavement lasts and looks planned.

Choosing by application

  • Green parking / fire lanes / sponge city: hollow permeable grass-grid hexagons (12.5/5 and 13.5/5.5) or the 48 cm holed slab — fill holes with soil+seed or gravel; build the open-graded sub-base first, otherwise the grass dies and the slab cracks.
  • Municipal sidewalks: 25–30 cm square/interlocking colored pavers 5–6 cm thick, plus tactile blister/strip tiles at crossings, stops and hazards (usually bright yellow).
  • Community roads / driveways: I/H-shaped and tooth/serrated interlockers 6–8 cm thick — the interlock transfers vehicle loads across joints.
  • Plazas, parks, courtyards: shaped (clover, crescent, bow, diamond, arrow) and patterned (maple leaf, plum blossom, chrysanthemum, Spanish, double-fan) decorative tiles — buy by the layout, not by the piece.
  • Resurfacing old pavement: thin 2–3 cm overlay pavers bonded onto a sound base — they are veneers, not structural slabs.

What to get right when ordering

  • Solid vs hollow: confirm whether the unit must carry grass/water (hollow) or just pave (solid). Most "colored pavers" are solid; only the permeable hexagons and holed slab in this range are hollow.
  • Four-piece motifs: plum blossom, chrysanthemum and other quarter-pattern tiles complete one flower per four tiles — order in multiples of four and fix pouring/laying direction.
  • Color: iron-oxide pigment mixed into the concrete; white cement for bright pastels. A pigmented wear-course layer saves pigment cost on thick tiles.
  • Cavities change the price: 1-out-9 makes nine tiles per pour — compare per-tile cost and daily output, not per mould.
  • Thickness = duty: 4–5 cm for foot traffic, 6 cm for community roads, 8 cm for occasional vehicle loads; don't pave driveways with 3 cm veneers.

Colored & Grass Paver Mould FAQ

What is the difference between a colored paver mould and a permeable/grass paver mould?

A standard colored paver (cai zhuan) mould casts a solid decorative or interlocking concrete tile for sidewalks and plazas. A permeable or grass paver mould casts a hollow unit with through holes: after laying, the holes are filled with soil and grass (or gravel), so the surface stays green while carrying light traffic — typical for parking bays, fire access lanes and sponge-city projects. In this catalogue only the hexagonal units (outer 12.5/13.5 cm with 5/5.5 cm inner holes) and the 48×24×8 cm serrated slab with two through holes are hollow; the shaped and patterned pavers are solid.

Can I use colored concrete in these moulds, and how is the color made?

Yes — that is why they are called colored paver moulds. Iron-oxide pigment is mixed into the concrete before pouring (or a pigmented wear-course layer is poured first, then the base concrete). Smooth ABS/PP faces release cleanly so the color stays even. White cement gives brighter pastel tones; grey cement with pigment gives deeper reds, yellows and greens.

How do I read dimensions like 25 x 25 x 5 cm or outer 12.5 / inner 5 cm?

For solid pavers it is length x width x thickness in centimetres. For permeable hexagons, the outer measurement is the hexagon's overall size (opposite-flat distance) and the inner measurement is the planting/drainage hole — a 12.5/5 unit has a 12.5 cm body with a 5 cm hole. Thin 2–3 cm units are overlay/veneer pavers for resurfacing existing pavements, not heavy-duty slabs.

Which paver shapes are best for driveways versus footways?

For driveways and fire lanes use hollow grass-grid/permeable units (filled and compacted) or thick interlocking I/H-shaped pavers (24–30 cm, 6 cm thick) whose joints transfer load. Footways, plazas and courtyards can use the decorative shaped and patterned pavers — clover, crescent, bow, diamond, maple-leaf and the four-piece flower patterns are designed to be combined into layouts, not to carry vehicle loads.

Do the four-piece flower patterns (plum blossom, chrysanthemum) come as one mould or four?

Each tile carries one quarter of the motif; four tiles laid together form one complete flower. Order in multiples of four pieces per full motif and plan the layout before pouring so the pattern direction is consistent. The same applies to the Spanish-style and double-fan continuous patterns.

What are tactile pavers and where are they required?

Tactile (blister/strip) pavers guide visually impaired pedestrians: raised dots (blister/warning tiles) mark hazards, stops and turns; long strips (directional tiles) mark the safe walking line. They are mandatory on municipal sidewalks, stations and public buildings. We supply both types in 20 × 20 × 6 cm and 25 × 25 × 5 cm in PP; color is usually bright yellow.

What does 1-out-2 or 1-out-9 mean?

It is the number of finished tiles one mould casts per pour — a 1-out-9 rough-stone mould makes nine 10 × 10 cm tiles in a single cycle. More cavities mean lower cost per tile for high-volume items. Compare quotes per finished tile and per daily output, not per mould.

PP or ABS for colored and decorative paver moulds?

PP (polypropylene) is the default and handles 300+ pour cycles with good flexibility for demoulding. ABS is chosen where deep crisp patterns, sharp arrises or textured faces (rough-stone, fine anti-slip grooves) must stay precise over many cycles — the fine detail releases more sharply in ABS. Tell us the pattern and expected volume and we will recommend the grade.

What is the MOQ and lead time?

Standard catalogue paver moulds have no strict MOQ and mixed-SKU orders are welcome. Lead time is typically 15–30 days; OEM shapes, logos or custom patterns depend on complexity. Send sizes, quantities and destination port for an FOB Ningbo/Shanghai quote.
