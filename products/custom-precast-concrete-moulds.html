Not every project uses catalogue sizes

Local standards, authority drawings, architect specifications, contractor-specific dimensions and branded logos often mean a standard catalogue mould will not produce the part you need. That is when you need a custom mould — built exactly to your section, your pattern and your project tolerance.

Five things that determine the quote — and where custom orders usually go wrong

A custom mould quote is only as good as the input. These are the five points that move price and lead time the most, and the mistakes we see most often from first-time OEM buyers.

  • Internal vs external dimensions. Drawings usually specify the finished concrete size (internal clear opening, finished depth). Moulds are cut to the external / as-cast dimension plus wall thickness and draft. If your drawing gives external dimensions only, tell us — otherwise we will build to the wrong number.
  • Wall thickness and draft angle. PP and ABS moulds need a draft (taper) to demould without tearing. A 1°–2° draft is normal for block / slab shapes; deeper parts may need more. If the part must have vertical walls (e.g. a tile with zero draft), that pushes us toward a split or multi-piece mould, which costs more and lasts longer.
  • Logos, letters and patterns. Embossed text (raised on the concrete) is much cheaper than debossed (engraved) because the mould is machined around the text. Deeply engraved logos, two-level patterns or fine serifs need an etched insert, which adds tooling cost. Send the artwork as AI / DXF / high-resolution PNG.
  • Material choice. The default PP mould gives 300+ pour cycles and is the cheapest. If the part is large, thin-walled, or will be used in sub-zero weather, upgrade to ABS or add ribs. PE is the right choice when the shape needs flex to demould (e.g. some interlocking blocks).
  • Sample vs drawing. A dimensioned CAD file is fastest. A photo with sizes written on it works. A physical sample mailed to the factory gives us the best chance to match texture and fit, but adds 5–7 days of courier time. If you have a sample, mail it — and send the drawing in parallel so we can start review.

Custom Precast Moulds — FAQ

Which file format do you need for a custom mould drawing?

DWG or DXF is preferred. PDF with dimensioned sections, STEP/IGES 3D files, or even a clear photo with sizes written on it in pen also work. For logo and pattern work, send vector artwork (AI / EPS / DXF) or a high-resolution PNG with clean edges.

Can you make a mould from a physical sample?

Yes. Mail the sample to our Jiaxing factory and we will 3D-scan or hand-measure it, confirm the drawing back to you, then cut the mould. Courier typically takes 5–7 days. Email the tracking number and a photo of the sample so we can start review in parallel.

What is the difference between ABS, PP and PE moulds?

PP is the default — lowest cost, 300+ pour cycles, slight flexibility for demoulding. ABS is harder, holds tighter tolerances and reproduces finer surface detail (logos, patterns) but is less impact-resistant in cold weather. PE is the most flexible and is used when the part has an undercut or shape that needs the mould to flex during stripping.

Can you emboss or engrave our company logo, project name or date code?

Yes. Embossed (raised) text and logos are included at low or no cost on most orders. Engraved (recessed) text, two-level patterns, or logos smaller than ~15 mm may need a machined / etched insert and add tooling cost. Send the artwork when you request the quote.

What is the lead time for a custom mould?

A simple profile change on an existing mould: 10–15 working days. A new shape with no existing tooling: 20–30 working days. Moulds with etched pattern inserts, complex split cavities or large sizes may run longer. The schedule is confirmed in writing after the deposit and drawing sign-off.

Do you make one-off prototype moulds, or only production quantities?

Single-piece prototype and first-article moulds are accepted. Many customers order one prototype first, pour a test batch, and then release a production order after the part is approved. Prototype moulds use the same material as the production version so the test is representative.

Can a custom mould be modified later if my dimension changes?

Some changes are possible (welding inserts to reduce a cavity, machining a deeper groove, adding a logo plate). Others are not (making a cavity larger, changing an external footprint, or adding an undercut). Tell us if the design is likely to be revised and we will build in extra stock so the mould can be re-machined.

How are custom moulds packed for export?

Moulds are stacked on pallets, layered with protective film, and strapped for 20 ft / 40 HC loading. Very large or fragile moulds get plywood crating. We mark each pallet with your PO number, mould dimensions and quantity for site identification. Packing photos are sent before container loading.
