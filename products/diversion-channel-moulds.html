Drainage Channel Mould Buyer Guide

Use this checklist to match drainage channel, U-drain and cover moulds to your local stormwater project before asking for a quotation.

Common project choices

  • Plain U-shaped diversion channels: open trough flow channels (30–62 cm lengths) for roadside drainage, irrigation ditches and stormwater lines.
  • Perforated / notched / coin-pattern slots: 3/5/6/8-hole downspout channels, end-notched connectors and decorative coin-pattern (铜钱) surface channels.
  • L-shaped diversion curb & gutter stones: L-section kerb-and-gutter units for residential community perimeter drainage, plain or perforated, with or without side holes.
  • Corner & water-stop fittings: inside/outside corner units and curved R50/R100/R150 barrier pieces that complete an L-shaped run.

Material and design notes

Most drainage mould buyers compare PP, ABS and PE according to concrete part size, expected reuse cycle, demoulding method and budget. For covers with many holes or narrow ribs, the mould structure should leave enough demoulding clearance and avoid weak corners.

  • Confirm finished concrete dimensions, not only mould outside size.
  • Check whether the cover needs anti-slip texture, logo or drainage slot direction.
  • For mixed orders, group U-drains, covers and catch basin parts by project line for easier packing.

Drainage Mould FAQ

Can you make diversion channel moulds from samples or drawings?

Yes. Send finished concrete dimensions (inner width × depth × length), wall thickness, hole or notch layout, drawings, sample photos or local standards. We review the mould structure and quote standard or custom production.

PP or ABS — which material should I choose for diversion channels?

Plain U-channels in normal sizes demould well in PP (lower cost, 300+ pour cycles). ABS is recommended for perforated / multi-hole downspout channels and L-shaped corner pieces: the narrow ribs between holes and the right-angle L-section need higher stiffness to survive repeated demoulding without chipping. Tell us the model and usage; we will suggest the material.

Are the holes in perforated channels drilled on site?

No. The 3/5/6/8-hole layout is moulded in — the holes form part of the concrete shape. Each hole count is a separate mould, and the spacing/slot depth (e.g. 2 cm slot depth on the 60×30×8 downspout channel) is fixed. Give us your pipe or slot layout first; do not plan to drill cured concrete, which cracks the section.

How do I specify the notches on notched / coin-pattern flow channels?

End notches (commonly 10×1.5 cm or 15×1.5 cm) let two channel runs connect or let water enter from the side. State notch width × depth and how many ends are open. A notch that is too wide leaves a loose joint; too narrow and the mating channel will not seat. Notched and coin-pattern versions of the same outer size (e.g. 30×30×6 or 60×30×6) are different moulds — order by the surface name printed on the product photo.

What is the difference between a U-shaped channel and an L-shaped gutter stone?

The U-channel is an open trough for a free-standing drainage line. The L-shaped gutter stone (L型水沟石 / 导流侧石) is an L-section kerb-and-gutter unit: the vertical back wall sits against a wall or road edge and the flat base carries the flow — it is the standard residential-community (小区) perimeter drainage unit. L-shaped pieces are available plain, perforated (6/8-hole downspout versions) or with/without side drain holes.

How do corner and water-stop pieces work for an L-shaped run?

Inside and outside corner units (30×30×6 for gutter stones, 30×15×6 for residential L-channels) turn a run 90°. Curved water-stop / barrier pieces (内挡水 / 外挡水) are made to fixed radii — commonly R50/R100 for inner barriers and R100/R150 for outer barriers. Like any curved precast part, one mould serves one radius: count inside and outside corners separately per plan, and give the radius when ordering curved barriers.

What concrete grade and wall thickness are recommended?

C25–C30 concrete with proper vibration and curing works for standard drainage components. Wall thickness (5–8 cm on most U-channels, 6–6.5 cm on L-gutter stones) is driven by backfill / traffic load, not by channel width — roadsides with vehicle load need thicker walls and reinforcement regardless of the hydraulic section.

Can one shipment mix U-channels, L-gutter stones, corners and covers?

Yes. Mixed-SKU container orders are common for municipal and residential drainage contractors. We recommend grouping components by project line (channel bodies + matched notches/holes + corner pairs + covers) so nothing is missing on site.
