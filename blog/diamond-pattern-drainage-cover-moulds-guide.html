Diamond Pattern Drainage Cover Moulds: Anti-Slip Cover Slab Buying Guide

How to choose diamond pattern drainage cover moulds for anti-slip concrete cover slabs, drainage trenches, sidewalks and municipal projects.

Diamond pattern drainage cover moulds are used to produce textured precast concrete cover slabs for drainage trenches, utility corridors, sidewalks, yards and municipal road edges. The surface pattern is not only decorative; it also helps create a visible anti-slip surface for pedestrian and maintenance areas.

Zhihua keeps the complete product-card images on the related diamond pattern product page so buyers can see the mould picture, Chinese catalogue title, dimensions and orange description notes together.

1. Confirm where the cover slab will be used

A cover for a light pedestrian drainage channel is different from a roadside or utility trench cover. Before asking for price, confirm whether the finished concrete slab is for sidewalks, courtyards, drainage trenches, municipal roads, landscaping areas or utility access covers.

2. Choose the right surface texture

Diamond pattern texture is useful when the buyer wants a clear anti-slip surface and an easy-to-recognize product style. If the project requires another surface, compare it with Dot Pattern Drainage Cover Moulds, slotted covers or custom-textured cover slabs.

3. Check size, thickness and edge form

For cover slab moulds, the critical dimensions are length, width, slab thickness, edge detail and any matching groove or shoulder that fits the drainage channel below. A small drawing or a sample photo with measurements can prevent quotation mistakes.

4. Decide catalogue mould or OEM mould

If a standard catalogue size matches your market, catalogue moulds are faster and more economical. If the cover must match a local drainage channel, road authority detail or project drawing, an OEM mould is safer.

5. What to send to Zhihua

  • Finished product length, width and thickness
  • Photo or drawing showing diamond pattern pattern requirements
  • Application and expected load environment
  • Quantity, destination country and packing preference
  • Logo, letters, numbers or local-standard marks if required

Frequently asked questions

Diamond pattern vs dot pattern — which anti-slip surface should I choose?

Both are anti-slip treads; the difference is visual and project-standard. Dots are round raised studs, the dominant choice on railway tunnel cable trenches; diamonds are interlocking rhombus ribs that read as a continuous directional tread, widely specified on municipal roadside and plaza trenches. Performance is set by slab thickness and reinforcement, not by the tread shape — choose the pattern your drawing or authority standard calls for. Both are moulded from the same PP/ABS tooling.

What sizes are in the diamond-pattern cover catalogue?

Standard municipal series (6 moulds): 44.4×49.4×3, 54.4×49.4×2.5, 59.4×49.5×6, 49.5×34.5×5, 54×50×5 and 84.4×49.4×3 cm. Heavy railway series (3 moulds): 100×50×8, 126×50×8 and the 148×50×7 diamond Tie Biao railway-standard cover. Dimensions are length × width × thickness in cm; any other size is made to drawing.

Does the diamond pattern affect the cover's load rating?

No — load comes from slab thickness and concrete reinforcement, not from the tread. The same diamond range includes 2.5-3 cm pedestrian covers and 7-8 cm railway covers. We do not label covers with a D/B load class: that is a property of the reinforced concrete you pour, not of the mould. Tell us the application (sidewalk, road shoulder or trackside) and we confirm the section thickness.

Does a diamond-pattern cover need a notch or holes?

Only where the trench demands it. A notch is moulded into the edge where a cable or pipe crosses the trench at that cover position — order it with notch width × depth and middle/end position; never cut it on site after casting. Holes are for water-carrying ditches where flow must pass through the slab. The standard diamond catalogue is solid slabs; send your drawing for notched or perforated covers.

Should I order a catalogue mould or an OEM mould?

If a standard catalogue size matches your market, catalogue moulds are faster and more economical. If the cover must match a local drainage channel, road-authority detail or project drawing — including project marks, logos or a different thickness — an OEM mould made from that drawing is safer and avoids fit mistakes on site.
